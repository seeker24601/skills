# Evaluate

Use for comparing alternatives, forecasting outcomes, or reviewing a proposal.

1. Identify the decision or resolvable outcome, criteria, constraints, horizon, and available alternatives. Keep the user's priorities distinct from factual uncertainty.
2. Load `forecast-and-options` for scenarios, probabilities, trade-offs, and sensitivity. Establish factual inputs through `research-and-fact-check`; use business or people analysis only where relevant.
3. Use `game-theory` for strategic responses and countermoves that change the comparison. Load `contrarian` when the user explicitly requests pushback, a stress test, or a scoped adversarial review. Otherwise keep balanced evaluation. Do not force objections or call your own skeptical pass independent review.
4. Compare the strongest options, including the status quo or a small reversible experiment where useful. State what evidence or preference changes the conclusion.
5. Deliver a conditional recommendation, forecast, or review verdict with its basis and consequential limits. Do not execute the decision, create commitments, or launch recurring monitoring without authorization.
