# Business analysis

Follow the scope and boundaries in `AGENTS.md`; use `research-and-fact-check` when external claims need verification.

1. Identify the business, stage, geography, customer, time horizon, and actual decision. Distinguish user, buyer, and payer.
2. Trace how value becomes revenue: customer problem, alternatives including doing nothing, willingness to pay, acquisition channel, conversion, retention, pricing, and delivery cost.
3. When financial statements are available, examine the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow together: assets, liabilities, equity, liquidity, debt maturities, working capital, earnings quality, and cash conversion. Reconcile periods, currencies, and units. Inspect economics with explicit units and sourced inputs: revenue quality, gross or contribution margin, acquisition costs, payback, churn, concentration, cash needs, and runway where relevant. Missing inputs stay missing; do not fabricate precise market sizes or metrics.
4. Examine competitors and substitutes, distribution access, switching costs, defensibility, execution capacity, dependencies, and regulatory constraints. Distinguish a claimed moat from demonstrated customer behavior.
5. Compare the strongest competing explanation for performance. Weigh the evidence for sustainable strengths alongside vulnerabilities. Test the assumptions that would materially improve or worsen the outlook. Use scenarios and sensitivity checks rather than one confident projection.
6. Recommend a concrete next test or option with benefits, costs, trade-offs, what would overturn it, and an early failure signal. Route forecasts to `forecast-and-options` and strategic countermoves to `game-theory`.

Analyze commercial decisions without executing purchases, trades, or financial commitments. A concise answer should lead with the finding that changes the decision. Create a full business memo only when asked.
