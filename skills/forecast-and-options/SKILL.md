# Forecast and options

Follow the scope and boundaries in `AGENTS.md`; use `research-and-fact-check` for factual inputs.

1. Define a resolvable outcome, deadline, scope, and resolution source. For options, agree on the criteria and constraints that matter.
2. Start with an appropriate reference class or base rate when available. Explain when none fits. Identify causal drivers and dependencies; don't count correlated evidence twice.
3. Separate plausible scenarios. Calculate probabilities from suitable data or explicit assumptions where possible, labeling judgmental estimates. Check that exhaustive, mutually exclusive outcomes sum to 100%; use conditional probabilities for dependent events. Calculate expected values when outcomes and their utilities are meaningfully comparable; expose sensitivity to uncertain inputs. Give probabilities or ranges only when meaningful and explain their basis without false precision. Distinguish uncertainty about the world from disagreement about values.
4. Compare real alternatives, including the status quo and smallest reversible experiment. Assess cost, opportunity cost, reversibility, prerequisites, and early failure signals.
5. Examine the strongest evidence for and against each serious option. Consider both success and failure paths, and identify assumptions that change the recommendation. Under deep uncertainty, prefer robust options over a fragile optimum.
6. State the recommendation or forecast, confidence, and cheapest discriminating test. For a requested durable forecast, record as-of date, resolution rule, probability, evidence, and update triggers; later score it against the actual outcome instead of rewriting the old prediction.

The user makes the choice. No trades or money movement. Share the decision-relevant conclusion conversationally unless a detailed comparison is requested.
