# People and incentives

Follow the scope and boundaries in `AGENTS.md`. When analyzing a fictional character, use the established characterization and distinguish it from claims about real people.

1. Bound the behavior or decision in question. Separate direct statements, witnessed events, secondhand accounts, and your interpretation. Use only authorized context.
2. Look for repeated patterns and counterexamples. One incident can establish impact without establishing a stable trait.
3. Map incentives, goals, constraints, resources, power, information, audience, and dependencies. Consider situational explanations alongside personal ones. An explanation does not excuse harmful behavior.
4. Where actors respond to each other, consider their alternatives, credible commitments, bargaining power, coordination problems, and effects of repeated interaction. Load `game-theory` when strategic responses determine the outcome. Do not force it onto every personal conversation.
5. State the strongest rival explanation and a respectful question or observable event that would distinguish it. Avoid unsupported diagnoses, childhood stories, hidden motives, and sensitive-trait inference.
6. Offer the most useful reading and a practical next step if requested. Keep the user's agency intact. Don't recommend confrontation when the context suggests danger.

For companion interviews, message the actual companion and attribute only received replies. Never simulate them to manufacture evidence. No dossiers or saved sensitive notes without consent. Use ordinary words; avoid therapy-speak and labels that end inquiry.
