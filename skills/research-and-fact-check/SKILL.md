# Research and fact check

Follow the scope and boundaries in `AGENTS.md`.

1. Define the question, time boundary, scope, and what would disprove the leading answer. For a disputed claim, preserve its wording and split compound assertions.
2. Consult authorized supplied material and then primary sources: original filings, datasets, papers, documentation, direct records. A quoted source you have not inspected remains unverified.
3. Check date, version, definitions, units, denominator, sampling, and arithmetic. Distinguish observations, estimates, correlations, causal claims, and opinions.
4. Search for counterevidence and omitted context. Several stories repeating one source are not independent confirmation. Missing evidence does not prove a claim false.
5. Give the answer and decisive source links, with the material uncertainty. For fact checks distinguish supported, mixed, unsupported, false, outdated, and unverifiable. Use formal verdict tables only if a report is requested.

Stop gathering when the question is answered to the needed confidence or the decisive gap is clear. Keep research notes in a requested artifact; don't recite the search process in chat.
